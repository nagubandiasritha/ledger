Everything Ledger can do

How to use Ledger

Ledger is a private money tracker that runs entirely in your browser. There's no account, no server, and no ads - your data never leaves your device. Here's everything it does.

Start here - the 3-minute setup
  1. Go to Accounts → + Add account and add where your money actually sits - checking, savings, cash, a credit card.
  2. Go to Transactions → Import and upload a bank or card statement (PDF or CSV), or add transactions by hand.
  3. Open Settings (⚙) → Backup & restore and download a backup. Do this now, and again every few weeks.

💰 Accounts & net worth

Each account is a real pool of money. Assets (checking, savings, cash, investments) count toward you; liabilities (credit cards, loans) count against you. Net worth = everything you own minus everything you owe.

  • Add an account: Accounts → + Add account. Pick a type, then enter what's in it (or owed on it) today.
  • Balances update themselves as you add transactions. You never edit a balance directly - it's always opening balance + money in − money out.
  • Set net worth: if you'd rather just state your true total, tap Set net worth and type it. Ledger adds one balancing entry called "Manual adjustment" and leaves your real accounts untouched.

Tip: enter your past transactions first, then set an account's current balance - that way the math anchors correctly.

📋 Transactions

Every record is one of three types, and choosing the right one is what keeps your numbers honest:

  • Expense - money leaving your world (groceries, rent).
  • Income - money arriving (salary, a refund).
  • Transfer - money moving between your own accounts (paying your credit card, funding savings, lending to a friend). Transfers never count as spending or income, so they don't distort your totals.
  • Add one: + Add transaction. Tick "Save & add another" to keep the form open and rattle off several in a row.
  • Duplicate warning: if a new entry matches one you already have (same date, amount, description), you'll get a heads-up before saving.
  • Two views: Grouped collapses repeat merchants with a ×N count and subtotals; Flat list is a plain table. Filter by account, category, search text, or a date range (presets like last 3 months / this year, or a custom From-To) - and the filters all combine.
  • See one account's activity: pick it in the All accounts filter, or hit Transactions on any card in the Accounts tab to jump straight there. That account's current balance appears in the totals bar. Transfers show under both accounts they touch.
  • Expand all / Collapse all: in Grouped view, one button opens or closes every category and repeat-merchant group at once. It works on whatever your filters are currently showing.
  • Edit fast: change a category straight from the dropdown in the list, or hit Edit for the full form.
  • Make a category on the spot: the category dropdown has ➕ New category… at the bottom - add one without leaving the form you're filling in.
  • Teach it as you go: after typing a description, tick “Always file … as …” and Ledger creates an auto-sort rule so that merchant files itself from then on.
  • Bulk edit: tick Select all (or individual rows), then set a category for all of them, move them to a different account, or delete them at once. Handy if a whole import landed in the wrong account - Select all, pick the right account, Move.
  • Undo: every delete shows an Undo button for a few seconds - including bulk deletes, accounts, goals and categories.

📄 Importing statements

Go to Transactions → Import statement, choose which account the statement belongs to, then upload it.

  • PDF - bank and credit-card statements are read automatically. No column matching needed.
  • CSV - you'll match up which column is the date, description and amount. Blank templates are available if you want to type your own.
  • Paste text - a fallback for anything else; paste rows and Ledger finds the date, description and amount.

In the review screen you can also fix things without starting over: pick ➕ New category… in any row's dropdown to create one on the spot, or hit the ⚡ button on a row to turn that merchant into an auto-sort rule - it re-files every matching row in the import and your existing records too.

Everything is auto-sorted using your rules and shown in a review screen first - nothing saves until you confirm. Likely duplicates are flagged and unticked automatically, so re-importing an overlapping statement won't double-count.

Note: importing into a credit card treats payments as transfers, not income - which is correct, since paying your card isn't earning money.

🏷 Categories & auto-sorting

  • Categories (Settings ⚙) - rename, recolor, add or delete your own. Deleting one moves its transactions to Uncategorized rather than losing them.
  • Auto-sort rules - map a keyword to a category, e.g. "kroger → Groceries". Ledger ships with a generic starter set (common shops, restaurants, bills, subscriptions) so imports sort themselves from day one; add your own to refine it.
  • Reset to defaults - the button by the Categories heading swaps in a clean generic set of categories and rules. Your transactions stay; any on a category that no longer exists move to Uncategorized, with an Undo.
  • Bulk add / remove - each section has a Bulk add button that takes a whole list at once (one category or rule per line), and tick-boxes plus Delete selected to remove many together. Both deletions have an Undo. In bulk rules, a category that doesn't exist yet is created for you.
  • Rules aren't fussy about exact wording: the words just need to appear somewhere in the description, in any order. "sun network" catches "SUN TV NETWORK LIM".
  • Adding a rule immediately re-sorts transactions you already have, and tells you how many it fixed.

📊 Overview & trends

  • Overview - net worth, money in, money out, and goal progress at a glance. Use the date picker to view a single month (with ‹ › to step), or pick a range: last 3/6 months, this year, all time, or a custom From-To.
  • Recurring monthly - Ledger spots your regular bills automatically (rent, internet, subscriptions) by finding charges that repeat across months at a similar amount, and totals what they cost you each month. Each one shows when the next charge is expected, and if a price has changed you'll see a badge like ↑ was $9.99 - handy for catching a subscription that quietly went up. Anything it gets wrong, hit ✕ to remove it from the list.
  • Trends tab - a donut of spending by category plus month-to-month in/out bars. Pick any date range (or custom From-To) to re-scope it. Click a category - on the donut or anywhere it appears - and a panel slides in (from the side on a tablet/desktop, up from the bottom on a phone) listing every transaction behind it, without leaving the page. Click a month bar to focus the donut on that month.
  • Net-worth trend: the Overview leads with your net worth as a big number and a line showing how it's moved over the last several months.
  • Categories over time: a What changed list ranks categories and shows each one's change versus the previous period (↑/↓ with % and amount), or switch to Monthly bars for a stacked view of where each month's money went.
  • Daily spending calendar: a real month grid - each day shows what you spent, shaded darker for heavier days, with a weekly total down the side. Step months with ‹ ›, and click any day to see that day's transactions.
  • Export: the ⭳ Export button on Transactions downloads whatever is currently filtered as a CSV (great for taxes or a spreadsheet).
  • Power search: type >100 or <20 in the search box to filter by amount, tick Hide transfers, or hit ⚑ Needs review to see everything still Uncategorized.
  • Keyboard: press N for a new transaction, / to jump to search, Esc to close a dialog.
  • Transfers are deliberately excluded from spending views, so moving your own money never looks like spending.

🎯 Budget - planning your month

The Budget tab is where you plan ahead instead of just recording the past.

  • Enter your monthly income at the top. Ledger shows how much of it you've assigned and how much is still free.
  • Set a budget for each category. Hit Use my averages to fill them from what you've actually been spending the last 3 months - a realistic starting point rather than a guess.
  • The table shows, per category, your usual spend, your budget, what you've spent this month, and how much is left (or how far over you are).
  • How past months went scores each recent month against your plan - under or over - and for the months you missed, lists the categories that blew it, biggest first. A ⚑ means that category was well above its own normal, so you can tell a one-off blowout from a budget that was simply set too low.
  • Use the ‹ › arrows to look at any month.

Note: past months are compared against your current plan, so changing a budget re-scores your history.

🎯 Goals

  • Create a goal with a target amount and an optional deadline - a trip, an emergency fund, anything.
  • Add money tops it up, and any savings-type transaction can be linked to a goal so it updates automatically.
  • Each goal shows a progress bar and tells you whether you're on pace for the date you set.

🏦 Debt payoff

  • Add a loan or card with its balance, interest rate (APR) and monthly payment.
  • Ledger works out when it's paid off, the debt-free date, and total interest, with a full month-by-month schedule you can expand.
  • Try raising the payment to see how much time and interest you'd save. It warns you if a payment is too small to ever clear the balance.

💾 Backups & your data

Your data is saved automatically on this device, in this browser. It stays there when you close and reopen the app. Nothing is ever uploaded anywhere.

  • Download backup (Settings ⚙) saves one file with everything - accounts, transactions, goals, rules, settings.
  • Restore from file loads it back, after showing you what's inside and asking to confirm.
  • That backup file is also how you move to a new device - there's no automatic syncing between devices.

Important: clearing your browser's data would erase the app's storage. Download a backup now and then - it's the only real safety net.

⚙️ Other bits

Open Settings (the gear, top right). It has two tabs: Categories & rules for how transactions get sorted, and Appearance for the colour theme and font. Backup & restore sits at the top, always in reach.

  • Themes & fonts - three colour themes (Forest Green, Warm Ledger, Midnight) and six typefaces, on the Appearance tab.
  • Currency - tap the currency button in the header to pick from ~50 world currencies (searchable). This changes how amounts are displayed; it does not convert existing values, so choose the one you actually track in and stick with it.

🤔 Common situations

I lent a friend $500. Add an account named after them with the type Owed to me, then log a Transfer from your checking into it. The transaction reads “Lent” and the account shows owed $500, so it's obvious you're waiting on that money. Your net worth doesn't change - the money is still yours, just parked. When they pay you back, transfer it the other way; it shows as “Repaid” and the account settles to zero. An Owed to you total appears on the Accounts tab whenever anyone owes you.

I paid my credit card. That's a Transfer from checking to the card account - not an expense. The original purchases already counted as spending.

I'm adding an old expense but my balance is already correct. Add the transaction first, then go to Accounts → Edit and re-enter today's balance. Ledger works backwards to keep both true.

I imported everything into the wrong account. Go to Transactions, tick Select all, choose the right account next to Move to, and hit Move. Transfers are skipped because they touch two accounts - open Edit on those individually. There's an Undo if you pick the wrong one.

I imported the same statement twice. Duplicates are detected and unticked in the review screen. If some slipped through, select them in the list and delete - and you've got Undo if you overshoot.

Good habits
  • Import a statement once a month rather than logging every purchase by hand.
  • Every time you see something land in Uncategorized, add a rule for it - the app gets smarter each month.
  • Download a backup after each big import.
  • Use Transfers for anything that isn't truly earning or spending. That single habit keeps every other number trustworthy.
